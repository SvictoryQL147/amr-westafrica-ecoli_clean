## ABSTRACT

**Background:** Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in *Escherichia coli* poses a critical public health threat across West Africa, yet genomic surveillance data from the region remains severely underrepresented in global databases. Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) combined with machine learning (ML) offers powerful tools for characterising resistance gene profiles and predicting multidrug resistance (MDR) phenotypes at scale.

**Methods:** We retrieved 235 *E. coli* genome assemblies from Nigeria (n=185) and Ghana (n=50) from the NCBI Assembly database. All genomes met quality thresholds (CheckM completeness ≥95%, contamination ≤5%). Resistome profiling was performed by k‑mer screening against curated resistance gene markers spanning 21 genes across 7 antibiotic classes. Independent MDR gold standard labels were obtained from NCBI AMRFinderPlus, defining MDR as resistance to ≥3 antibiotic classes. Random Forest and XGBoost classifiers were trained on resistance gene presence/absence profiles to predict these independent MDR labels using stratified 5‑fold cross‑validation. Model interpretability was assessed using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP).

**Results:** Resistance genes were detected in 154/235 (65.5%) genomes. The most prevalent resistance gene was blaTEM (55.3%), followed by sul2 (48.5%) and qnrS (26.8%). ESBL-encoding blaCTX-M was detected in 4.3% of isolates, and carbapenem resistance gene blaNDM-1 was identified in two Nigerian isolates (0.9%). Using independent AMRFinderPlus labels, overall MDR prevalence was 52.8% (124/235). Random Forest achieved a mean ROC‑AUC of 0.954 (±0.011) and accuracy of 0.915 (±0.013); XGBoost achieved a mean ROC‑AUC of 0.954 (±0.009) and accuracy of 0.915 (±0.013). SHAP analysis identified blaTEM, sul2, and qnrS as primary predictors of MDR. Notably, no WGS data were available from Senegal or Cameroon, highlighting a critical regional surveillance gap.

**Conclusions:** This study provides the first comprehensive resistome and ML‑based MDR analysis of *E. coli* from West Africa, revealing high prevalence of clinically critical resistance genes including evidence of carbapenem resistance emergence. The realistic ML performance demonstrates that a compact gene panel can predict MDR without circularity. The severe underrepresentation of West African genomic data underscores an urgent need for investment in regional sequencing and bioinformatics capacity.

**Keywords:** antimicrobial resistance, *Escherichia coli*, West Africa, Nigeria, Ghana, whole-genome sequencing, machine learning, resistome, multidrug resistance, blaNDM-1
